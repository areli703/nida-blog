# How to Start a Marketing Agency in 2026: What to Do in Your First 90 Days

Everyone with a laptop and a Canva account has thought about starting a marketing agency. The barrier to entry is low, the demand is real, and the income ceiling is essentially unlimited. But the failure rate is also high — not because the business model is broken, but because most people skip the foundation and start selling services they can't deliver.

This post is a 90-day blueprint. No motivational speeches. No "just hustle" advice. Just the specific steps, tools, costs, and pricing that separate agencies that survive from agencies that fold in six months.

---

## What You Actually Need (Versus What the Gurus Sell You)

You do not need an LLC on day one. You do not need a $5,000 website. You do not need a team, a funnel, or a course. You need three things:

1. **One service you can sell confidently.**
2. **One channel to find clients predictably.**
3. **One way to deliver that service without burning out.**

Everything else is decoration. The agencies that fail in month three are usually the ones that tried to offer SEO, PPC, web design, social media, branding, and email marketing on day one. They confuse prospects, dilute their message, and guarantee mediocre results.

Pick one offer. Get good at it. Add services later from a position of strength, not desperation.

## The Real Costs of Starting a Marketing Agency in 2026

Here is what you actually spend in your first three months:

| Expense Category | Bare Minimum | Smart Investment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business registration / LLC** | $0 (operate as sole prop) | $50–$500 | Not urgent until revenue flows |
| **Website / landing page** | $0 (Notion or Carrd) | $29–$149/mo (white-label builder) | You are selling marketing; your site matters |
| **CRM / client management** | $0 (Notion, Google Sheets) | $29–$99/mo | Critical after client #3 |
| **Email + calendar** | $0 (Gmail, Google Calendar) | $6–$12/mo (Google Workspace) | Professional email matters more than most think |
| **Design tools** | $0 (Canva free) | $13–$20/mo (Canva Pro, Figma) | Needed for social and ad creative |
| **Ad spend (optional)** | $0 | $300–$1,000/mo | Cold outreach is free; ads accelerate once you have proof |
| **Software / white-label platform** | $0 | $29–$149/mo | Only after you know your offer |

**Total first 90 days:** $0–$200 if you bootstrap. $500–$2,000 if you invest in tools and ads. Either path works. The agency that spends more does not necessarily win. The agency that spends *intentionally* does.

## The 90-Day Timeline

### Week 1–2: Foundation

- Pick your niche. "Local businesses" is too broad. "Dental practices in Phoenix" or "HVAC companies" is specific enough to message effectively.
- Pick your service. Website design, local SEO, paid ads, or social media management. Choose the one you can deliver well with your current skills.
- Set up a basic online presence. A Carrd landing page with a clear headline, offer description, social proof placeholder, and contact form is enough.
- Get a professional email. `yourname@gmail.com` kills credibility. Google Workspace is $6/month.

### Week 3–4: Build Your Offer

- Create a standardized package. "I build local business websites with SEO and hosting for $197/month" is a product. "I do web stuff" is not.
- Define your process. Map out every step from first contact to delivery. If you don't know what step three is, you'll panic when a client says yes.
- Set your price. Undercharge to get your first 2–3 clients. Overcharge once you have results to reference. The first client is market research, not profit.

### Week 5–8: Outreach

- Send 20 personalized outreach messages per day. Use LinkedIn, email, or Instagram DMs. Personalize every single one. "I noticed your website isn't mobile-friendly" is 100x better than "I do marketing services."
- Follow up twice. Most responses come on the second or third touch, not the first.
- Track everything. Who you contacted, when, what they said. A simple spreadsheet prevents leads from falling through cracks.

### Week 9–12: Close and Deliver

- Close your first 2–5 clients. Focus on speed of delivery. A website live in 7 days beats a perfect website in 30 days.
- Ask for a testimonial or case study. Social proof is the fuel for every future sale.
- Analyze what worked. Which outreach channel produced replies? Which offer got the most interest? Double down.

## Service Pricing That Actually Sells

New agencies either price too high (no portfolio) or too low (no margin). Here is a realistic pricing ladder:

| Service Tier | Monthly Price | What's Included | Who Buys It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $97–$197/mo | One-page website, hosting, monthly edits | Solo business owners, tradespeople |
| **Growth** | $297–$497/mo | Multi-page website, SEO basics, lead capture | Established local businesses with budget |
| **Premium** | $597–$997/mo | Full site + CRM + automated follow-up + reporting | Businesses that value turnkey systems |

The mistake is asking the client what they want. Clients don't know what they want. You tell them: "For $297/month, you get this, this, and this." Package pricing outsells hourly pricing because it shifts the conversation from cost to value.

---

## Common First-Year Mistakes

- **Chasing every platform.** You do not need GoHighLevel, HubSpot, Monday.com, and Zapier on day one. Start with one tool that handles your core need.
- **Custom scope on every project.** Productized services scale. Custom projects trap you in endless revisions.
- **Spending before earning.** It is tempting to buy $500/month in SaaS before you have $500/month in revenue. Resist.
- **No follow-up system.** 80% of sales happen after the fifth contact. If you send one email and give up, you are not in business.

What is harder is the discipline to focus on one service, one niche, and one outreach channel until they work.
